Module Overview

Understanding Security and Risk Management

Security and risk are increasingly interconnected within modern organisations. As enterprises adopt increasingly integrated digital systems, cybersecurity risk must be considered alongside wider operational, financial, regulatory and strategic risks.

This module develops an understanding of the methods used to identify, analyse, evaluate and treat cybersecurity risks. It considers both qualitative and quantitative risk assessment approaches and examines how different methods may be appropriate in different organisational contexts.

The module also explores established threat modelling approaches including STRIDE, DREAD, Attack Trees and PASTA, together with tools and resources that support threat analysis and vulnerability prioritisation.

Additional areas of study include security and regulatory standards, quantitative risk modelling, business continuity, disaster recovery and emerging trends within the wider field of security and risk management.

Units 3–4

Threat Modelling and Management

Units 3 and 4 develop the distinction between threats and vulnerabilities and introduce established methods for representing potential attacks and prioritising security risks.

01

STRIDE

A structured technique for identifying threats involving spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial of service and elevation of privilege.

02

DREAD

A risk-rating approach traditionally used to assess and prioritise the severity of identified threats.

03

Attack Trees

A graphical technique for representing an attacker's primary objective and the possible pathways through which that objective may be achieved.

04

PASTA

A risk-centred threat modelling methodology designed to connect business objectives, technical analysis and possible attack scenarios.

05

MITRE ATT&CK

A knowledge base that supports analysis of adversary tactics, techniques and behaviour during threat modelling.

06

OWASP and CVSS

Resources that assist with vulnerability understanding, threat analysis and prioritisation.

My Reflection

Threat modelling has strengthened my understanding that identifying vulnerabilities alone is not sufficient. Effective security analysis must also consider attack objectives, likely pathways, dependencies, attacker behaviour and possible consequences.

Units 7–8

Quantitative Risk Modelling

These units move beyond qualitative assessment and introduce mathematical, probabilistic and multi-criteria approaches to representing uncertainty and risk.

01

Monte Carlo Simulation

Monte Carlo methods use repeated simulations to represent uncertainty and estimate the distribution of possible outcomes.

02

Bayesian Methods

Bayesian approaches use probability and available evidence to update estimates of uncertainty and risk.

03

Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis

Techniques such as TOPSIS, AHP and ANP support decision-making where several competing criteria influence the final risk-management decision.

Units 9 and 10 focus on organisational resilience and the design of appropriate recovery strategies following disruption.

BIA

Business Impact Assessment

Identifying critical organisational functions and evaluating the consequences of disruption.

RTO

Recovery Time Objective

Defining the target period within which a disrupted service or system should be restored.

RPO

Recovery Point Objective

Defining the acceptable amount of data loss, measured in time, following disruption.

DR

Disaster Recovery Design

Selecting technical and organisational recovery arrangements that meet defined recovery requirements.

DRaaS

Disaster Recovery as a Service

Evaluating externally provided recovery services, including their benefits, limitations and dependencies.

R

Resilience

Considering redundancy, vendor lock-in, network security and other resilience factors within disaster recovery planning.

Reflective Practice

Critical Reflection

This module is developing my understanding of cybersecurity from a broader risk-management perspective. Earlier technical work naturally encouraged me to focus primarily on vulnerabilities, systems and defensive technologies. Security and Risk Management has required me to consider how technical weaknesses relate to organisational risk, uncertainty and business consequences.

The distinction between qualitative and quantitative risk assessment has been particularly important. Qualitative approaches can support rapid and understandable prioritisation, whereas quantitative approaches can provide numerical estimates and represent uncertainty in greater detail. However, their value depends upon the quality of evidence, assumptions and data available.

Threat modelling has strengthened my ability to think from an attacker's perspective. Rather than considering individual vulnerabilities in isolation, techniques such as STRIDE, Attack Trees and PASTA encourage consideration of attack objectives, possible pathways, system dependencies and likely consequences.

The work on security standards has also shown me that compliance alone does not guarantee effective cybersecurity. Standards and regulatory requirements provide important governance structures, but organisations must still understand their specific threats, assets and risk environment.

Quantitative risk modelling has introduced a more analytical approach to uncertainty. Techniques such as Monte Carlo simulation and Bayesian analysis have demonstrated how risk can be represented and evaluated numerically, although the outputs remain dependent on assumptions and the quality of input data.

Business continuity and disaster recovery have further broadened my understanding of cybersecurity. Security is not only about preventing compromise; organisations must also prepare for disruption, establish recovery priorities and ensure that essential services can continue or be restored within acceptable timeframes.

Overall, the module is helping me connect technical cybersecurity analysis with organisational decision-making, resilience, regulation, uncertainty and professional judgement. This is particularly relevant to my continuing development towards a professional career in cybersecurity.
