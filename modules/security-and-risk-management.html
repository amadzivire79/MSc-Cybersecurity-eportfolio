MODULE OVERVIEW

Understanding Security and Risk

Security and Risk Management has developed my understanding of cybersecurity as an organisational risk management responsibility rather than simply a technical problem.

The module has encouraged me to consider how information assets, people, technology, business processes and external dependencies interact to create cybersecurity risk.

I have also developed an understanding of how structured frameworks and standards can support organisations in identifying risks, implementing security controls and improving their overall cybersecurity posture.

04 • REFLECTION

My Security and Risk Management Reflection

Security and Risk Management has changed my understanding of cybersecurity by demonstrating that security cannot be treated solely as a technical problem. Effective cybersecurity requires an understanding of organisational objectives, critical assets, people, processes, technologies and the risks created by their interdependencies.

One of the most important lessons I have developed is the distinction between a threat, a vulnerability and a risk. A threat represents a potential cause of harm, while a vulnerability can provide an opportunity for that threat to be realised. Risk requires consideration of both the likelihood of an adverse event and its potential consequences.

My study of threat modelling has helped me develop a more structured way of thinking about cybersecurity. Instead of considering individual vulnerabilities in isolation, I have learned to consider assets, trust relationships, attack surfaces, potential attack paths and security controls as interconnected components of a wider system.

This was particularly evident when examining cyber-physical systems. Such environments demonstrate that cybersecurity incidents can have consequences beyond information loss because attacks may affect physical processes, operational continuity and safety. This has strengthened my appreciation of the need to consider cybersecurity in the context of the wider system.

Studying frameworks such as the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and ISO/IEC 27001 has also helped me understand the importance of structured governance. Frameworks provide useful ways of organising security activities, but their effectiveness depends on how well they are implemented within an organisation's specific risk environment.

I have also developed a greater appreciation of the relationship between technical and organisational controls. Firewalls, authentication, vulnerability management and monitoring are important, but they need to operate alongside policies, procedures, training, governance and effective risk ownership.

How My Thinking Has Changed

I now approach cybersecurity problems by first asking what needs to be protected, why it needs protection, what could threaten it, which vulnerabilities may be exploited and what the consequences would be. This approach has made my analysis more structured and risk-focused.

Connection to My Professional Development

My professional experience in fibre infrastructure has also given me a practical context for considering security risk. Telecommunications networks support critical connectivity, and their security depends on physical infrastructure, network equipment, configurations, access controls, software, users and operational processes.

This has increased my interest in investigating how cybersecurity risk can be assessed and managed across last-mile telecommunications environments, particularly where physical infrastructure and digital systems interact.

What I Will Do Differently

Going forward, I will apply a more systematic risk-based approach to cybersecurity problems. I will also strengthen my ability to justify security recommendations using recognised frameworks, evidence and appropriate risk analysis rather than relying only on technical assumptions.
