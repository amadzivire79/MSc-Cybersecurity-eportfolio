1. Module Overview

Launch into Computing introduces the theoretical, practical and professional foundations of contemporary computing. The module examines the relationship between computational principles, software engineering, data science, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and enterprise information technology.

The module also addresses the legal, ethical and professional responsibilities associated with computing, including data protection, digital inclusion, secure systems and responsible technological innovation.

This portfolio organises the module's learning activities and identifies opportunities to demonstrate technical competence, critical analysis and reflective professional development.

2. Learning Outcomes

The following capabilities are drawn together from the unit-level outcomes.

  • Analyse computing challenges and processes in business systems.
  • Apply computational logic, Boolean algebra, algorithms and data structures.
  • Evaluate software engineering principles and development approaches.
  • Investigate data science, AI and enterprise storage systems.
  • Assess cybersecurity threats, risk management and resilience.
  • Critically evaluate IT governance frameworks and strategic alignment.
  • Explain legal, ethical, social and professional responsibilities.
  • Evaluate emerging computing technologies and future trends.

3. Unit-by-Unit Learning Portfolio

Each unit identifies the principal areas of learning, the activity specified in the module materials and suitable evidence for inclusion.

Unit 1

What is Computing?

Introduction to computing disciplines, digital transformation, business innovation and equality, diversity and inclusion.

Artefacts: AI-powered assistants case study, introductory Python exercises, GitHub registration evidence and initial collaborative discussion contribution.

Reflection focus: The relationship between computing, responsible innovation and enterprise transformation.

Unit 2

Logical Foundations of Computing

Boolean algebra, truth tables, logical gates and set theory, including their use in circuits and computational systems.

Artefacts: Boolean logic case study, logic circuit design, truth tables and collaborative discussion contributions.

Reflection focus: Applying mathematical reasoning to logical decision-making and system design.

Unit 3

Principles of Computer Science

Algorithms, searching, sorting, data structures and computational complexity.

Artefact: Algorithm Analysis and Implementation activity, including program code and analytical findings.

Reflection focus: Comparing algorithm efficiency, scalability and suitability for computational problems.

Unit 4

Software Engineering

Software Development Life Cycle, requirements, implementation, testing, maintenance, Waterfall, Agile and DevOps.

Artefacts: Comparative Analysis of Software Development Methodologies and collaborative discussion summary contribution.

Reflection focus: Selecting appropriate development methodologies and considering software quality and sustainability.

Unit 5

Data Science and Storage

Python data analytics, statistical techniques, SQL, NoSQL, cloud storage, structured and unstructured data.

Artefacts: Data Science Techniques case study and Data Analytics with Python and Data Storage Reflection.

Reflection focus: The importance of data quality, storage selection, data privacy and ethical analysis.

Unit 6

Principles of Artificial Intelligence I

Artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, automation and predictive analytics.

Artefact: Implementing and Evaluating a Simple AI Model.

Reflection focus: AI model performance, reliability, transparency and potential algorithmic bias.

Unit 7

Principles of Artificial Intelligence II

Continued evaluation of AI applications, technical risks, enterprise opportunities and responsible implementation.

Artefacts: Completion of the AI model activity where required, and the Security Risk Assessment Report.

Reflection focus: Ethical and security implications of implementing AI-supported systems.

Unit 8

Principles of Cyber Security

Cyber threats, vulnerabilities, threat modelling, encryption, risk assessment and security governance.

Artefact: Cybersecurity Threat Assessment and Mitigation Plan.

Reflection focus: Applying risk-based approaches to protecting confidentiality, integrity and availability.

Unit 9

Computing and Risk

Strategic IT governance, enterprise architecture, digital transformation, COBIT, ITIL and TOGAF.

Artefact: Disaster Recovery Plan Development.

Reflection focus: Aligning IT management, organisational risk and business continuity requirements.

Unit 10

Strategic and Enterprise IT Management

Operational IT governance, risk management, compliance, disaster recovery and IT-business alignment.

Artefacts: Toyota Digital Transformation case study discussion and IT Governance Framework Analysis.

Reflection focus: Translating enterprise technology strategy into operational controls.

Unit 11

Future-Proofing IT Systems

Organisational resilience, socio-technical risks, ethical governance, regulatory obligations and long-term risk management.

Artefacts: Developing a Resilient and Strategic IT Framework and the Future Trends blog post activity.

Reflection focus: Integrating technical resilience, responsible governance and professional accountability.

Unit 12

Future Trends and Concepts in Computing

Quantum computing, blockchain, artificial intelligence, edge computing, cybersecurity and sustainable innovation.

Artefacts: End-of-module Programming Exercise and Presentation, together with supporting source code and presentation evidence.

Reflection focus: Emerging opportunities, technical challenges, professional responsibilities and future development priorities.
