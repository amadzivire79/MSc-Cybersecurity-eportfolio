Purpose of My Learning Journal

This reflective journal provides a structured record of my academic and professional development throughout the MSc Cybersecurity programme. It captures important learning experiences from practical exercises, seminar activities, academic reading, assessments, collaborative work and independent investigation.

My reflections focus not only on what I completed, but also on how my understanding changed, the challenges I encountered, the significance of my findings and how each learning experience contributes to my continuing development as a cybersecurity professional.

02 SEP 2026
Security & Risk Management

GDPR Case Study Analysis

Examining information security responsibilities, organisational controls and regulatory compliance.

01

Activity

I reviewed a Data Protection Commission case study concerning the handling of personal information and considered the relevant data protection principles, the organisation's response and the measures that an Information Security Manager could implement to reduce the likelihood of a similar incident.

02

Key Learning

The activity demonstrated that effective information security requires more than technical safeguards. Governance, staff awareness, clearly defined procedures, accountability and risk management all contribute to the protection of personal information.

03

Critical Reflection

My initial understanding of data protection focused mainly on preventing unauthorised access or disclosure. The case study broadened my perspective by showing that weaknesses in organisational processes and decision-making can create significant information security risks even when technical controls are available.

04

Professional Relevance

As a future cybersecurity professional, this reinforces the importance of assessing people, processes and technology together rather than treating information security as a purely technical discipline.

05

Next Step

I intend to explore how recognised security frameworks such as ISO/IEC 27001 and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework can support organisations in managing information security and data protection obligations systematically.

20 AUG 2026
Network Security

Vulnerability Scanning and Network Reconnaissance

Developing practical understanding of network visibility, attack surfaces and risk-based vulnerability management.

01

Activity

I completed practical network security activities involving tools and techniques such as Nmap, Nessus, traceroute, dig and nslookup. These exercises were used to gather information about network hosts, services, software and potential vulnerabilities.

02

Results and Observations

The exercises demonstrated how information about open ports, running services, network paths and software configurations can provide valuable information for identifying potential attack surfaces.

03

Critical Reflection

Initially, I viewed vulnerability scanning primarily as a method of identifying technical weaknesses. However, I increasingly recognised that scan results require interpretation. Severity ratings alone do not necessarily determine organisational risk.

Factors such as exploitability, exposure, asset value, business impact and existing controls must also be considered when deciding remediation priorities.

04

Professional Relevance

This activity strengthened my interest in network security and vulnerability management. It demonstrated how technical investigation can support risk-based cybersecurity decision-making.

05

Next Step

I plan to develop greater confidence in analysing CVEs, validating vulnerability scanner findings and recommending proportionate remediation measures based on technical and organisational risk.

15 JUL 2026
Launch into Computing

Developing Technical Confidence and Computational Thinking

Reflecting on the development of computational problem-solving and practical technical skills.

01

Activity

During Launch into Computing, I engaged with practical computing activities that required structured problem-solving, interpretation of technical information and the use of computing tools to investigate problems.

02

Reflection

One of the most important developments from this module was greater confidence in approaching unfamiliar technical tasks. Rather than expecting an immediate solution, I began to approach problems systematically by identifying the objective, breaking the task into smaller stages, testing possible solutions and evaluating the outcome.

03

Professional Development

This structured problem-solving approach is highly relevant to cybersecurity, where practitioners regularly encounter incomplete information, unfamiliar systems and rapidly evolving technical challenges.

04

Next Step

I will continue strengthening my practical computing, scripting and analytical skills and apply them to more advanced network security and cybersecurity investigations.
