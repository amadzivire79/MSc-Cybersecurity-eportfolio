Module Overview

Understanding Security as Organisational Risk

Security and Risk Management extends cybersecurity beyond purely technical controls by examining how organisations identify, evaluate, prioritise and manage uncertainty.

The module has encouraged me to consider cybersecurity as a socio-technical discipline in which technology, people, organisational processes, governance, regulation, legal obligations and ethical responsibilities interact.

Through academic discussions, case studies, group activities and risk-based exercises, I am developing a more systematic approach to understanding assets, threats, vulnerabilities, likelihood, consequences and appropriate risk treatment.

Reflective Practice

My Reflection

This module is changing how I understand cybersecurity. Earlier technical work naturally encouraged me to focus primarily on vulnerabilities, systems and defensive controls. Security and Risk Management has required me to consider the broader organisational context in which those vulnerabilities exist.

I am developing a stronger appreciation of the distinction between a vulnerability and a risk. A technical weakness does not have the same significance in every environment. Its importance depends upon the asset affected, possible threat actors, likelihood of exploitation and potential business consequences.

Threat modelling has reinforced the importance of understanding interdependencies. Security failures can propagate across interconnected systems, meaning that risk analysis must consider pathways and consequences rather than examining components in isolation.

Governance frameworks have also helped me understand that effective cybersecurity requires leadership, accountability, monitoring and continual improvement. Technical controls are essential, but they must form part of a broader risk-management process.

Collaborative activities have additionally reinforced the importance of communication and professional judgement. Working with other students exposed me to alternative interpretations of risk and demonstrated the importance of supporting decisions with evidence.

Overall, I am becoming more confident in connecting technical security analysis with governance, organisational priorities, ethics and professional judgement.
