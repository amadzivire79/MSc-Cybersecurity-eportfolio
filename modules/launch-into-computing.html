01

Module Overview

Launch into Computing

Launch into Computing introduces the theoretical, practical and professional foundations of contemporary computing. The module examines the relationship between computational principles, software engineering, data science, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and enterprise information technology.

The module also addresses the legal, ethical and professional responsibilities associated with computing, including data protection, digital inclusion, secure systems and responsible technological innovation.

This portfolio organises the module's learning activities and identifies opportunities to demonstrate technical competence, critical analysis and reflective professional development.

02

Learning Outcomes

Knowledge and Capabilities

The following capabilities are drawn together from the unit-level outcomes.

  • Analyse computing challenges and processes in business systems.
  • Apply computational logic, Boolean algebra, algorithms and data structures.
  • Evaluate software engineering principles and development approaches.
  • Investigate data science, AI and enterprise storage systems.
  • Assess cybersecurity threats, risk management and resilience.
  • Critically evaluate IT governance frameworks and strategic alignment.
  • Explain legal, ethical, social and professional responsibilities.
  • Evaluate emerging computing technologies and future trends.

07

Critical Reflection

Reflecting on My Learning

This reflection considers the computing concepts, practical activities and professional capabilities developed throughout Launch into Computing.

7.1 Developing Computational Thinking

The early units introduced mathematical and logical principles that underpin software development and computational problem-solving. Boolean logic, algorithms and data structures provide useful foundations for designing efficient systems and understanding the behaviour of computing processes.

7.2 Programming and Software Engineering

Python programming and software engineering activities offer opportunities to move from theoretical understanding to practical implementation. Evaluating software development methodologies also illustrates the importance of requirements, testing, documentation and maintainability.

7.3 Data Science and Artificial Intelligence

Data science and AI activities introduce the relationship between data quality, computational models and decision-making. They also raise important concerns about bias, privacy, accuracy and responsible technological implementation.

7.4 Cybersecurity and Risk Management

Cybersecurity, disaster recovery and risk management activities connect computing knowledge to the protection and resilience of organisational systems. They also demonstrate why technical safeguards must be supported by governance, business continuity planning and ethical decision-making.

7.5 Enterprise Governance and Future Trends

Enterprise governance frameworks highlight the importance of aligning IT decisions with strategic objectives. Emerging technologies further demonstrate the need for continuous professional development and critical evaluation of innovation.

7.6 Overall Reflective Evaluation

The module connects computational thinking, programming, data analysis, artificial intelligence and governance with cybersecurity practice. Its interdisciplinary foundation supports subsequent study in network security, risk management and secure software development. Continued practice and critical evaluation remain important for translating these foundations into professional competence.
