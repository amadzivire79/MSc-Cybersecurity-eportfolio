Reflective Learning Journal

A chronological record of my learning, observations, results, challenges, feedback and professional development throughout my MSc Cybersecurity programme.

Purpose of this Journal

This reflective journal documents the development of my knowledge, technical skills and professional practice throughout the MSc Cybersecurity programme. It provides an opportunity to critically reflect on academic activities, practical exercises, seminar discussions, assessment feedback and independent research.

Entries focus not only on what I completed, but also on what I learned, the challenges encountered, how my understanding developed, and how the experience may influence my future academic and professional practice.

02 September 2026 Security and Risk Management

GDPR Case Study Analysis

Activity

I reviewed a Data Protection Commission case study concerning data protection and considered the specific GDPR principles involved, how the issue was resolved, and how an Information Security Manager could mitigate similar risks.

My Initial Thoughts

My initial understanding of GDPR compliance focused strongly on preventing unauthorised access to or disclosure of personal data. The activity encouraged me to consider data protection more broadly, including organisational procedures, accountability, staff awareness and governance.

Key Learning

One of my main observations was that effective information security cannot depend entirely on technical controls. Organisations must combine appropriate technologies with policies, procedures, employee awareness, risk management and effective governance.

Reflection

The exercise strengthened my understanding of the relationship between cybersecurity, information security governance and data protection legislation. As an Information Security Manager, I would therefore consider both technical vulnerabilities and weaknesses in organisational processes when assessing information security risk.

Ideas and Questions

This activity raised an interesting question for further investigation: how effectively can recognised information security frameworks such as ISO/IEC 27001 and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework support organisations in meeting their data protection obligations?

Next Steps

I intend to explore the relationship between security frameworks, risk management controls and GDPR requirements further during this module.

August 2026 Network Security

Vulnerability Scanning and Network Reconnaissance

Activity

I undertook practical network-security activities using tools and techniques including Nmap, Nessus, traceroute, dig and nslookup to investigate network infrastructure and identify potential security weaknesses.

Results and Observations

The exercises demonstrated how information about hosts, network services, open ports and software configurations can contribute to identifying potential attack surfaces.

Reflection

The practical work helped me understand that vulnerability scanning is not simply about producing a list of vulnerabilities. Results must be interpreted within the context of the system, the potential threat, likelihood of exploitation and possible organisational impact.

Professional Relevance

This learning is particularly relevant to my developing interest in network security because it demonstrates how network visibility, vulnerability management and risk assessment contribute to a defence-in-depth security strategy.

Next Steps

I intend to continue developing my ability to interpret vulnerability scan results, investigate relevant CVEs and recommend proportionate remediation measures.
