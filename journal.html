MODULE 03
Security and Risk Management Academic Reflection

GDPR Case Study Analysis

Reflecting on data protection responsibilities, organisational accountability and security risk.

01

Activity

I reviewed a Data Protection Commission case study to identify the relevant GDPR issue, understand how the matter was resolved and consider how an Information Security Manager could reduce the likelihood of recurrence.

02

Key Learning

The activity strengthened my understanding that data protection requires more than technical safeguards. Governance, staff awareness, accountability and effective organisational processes are equally important.

03

Critical Reflection

I initially approached GDPR mainly as a compliance requirement. The case study helped me recognise that privacy failures can arise from weaknesses in organisational behaviour, procedures and decision-making as much as from technical vulnerabilities.

04

Professional Relevance

This learning is relevant to information security management because cybersecurity decisions must consider legal obligations, data governance, risk management and stakeholder trust.

05

Next Step

I will continue developing my understanding of GDPR alongside recognised security frameworks so that I can better connect legal compliance with practical security controls.

MODULE 02
Network Security Technical Reflection

Vulnerability Scanning and Network Reconnaissance

Reflecting on the use of security tools to identify and interpret network vulnerabilities.

01

Activity

I used tools including Nessus, Nmap, traceroute, dig and nslookup to investigate network characteristics and identify potential vulnerabilities.

02

Key Learning

I developed a stronger appreciation of how network reconnaissance and vulnerability scanning can reveal information about exposed services, technologies and potential attack surfaces.

03

Critical Reflection

The exercises showed me that automated tools are valuable, but their findings still require interpretation. A scanner may identify a vulnerability, but understanding its context, severity and practical risk requires analytical judgement.

04

Professional Relevance

These activities strengthened practical skills relevant to vulnerability management, network defence and security assessment.

05

Next Step

I intend to develop greater confidence in prioritising vulnerabilities by combining technical findings with risk, exploitability and business impact.

MODULE 01
Launch into Computing Development Reflection

Developing Technical Confidence and Computational Thinking

Reflecting on the development of problem-solving, technical reasoning and computing confidence.

01

Activity

I completed introductory computing activities involving problem-solving, computational thinking, data handling and technical analysis.

02

Key Learning

The module helped me develop a more systematic approach to breaking complex problems into smaller tasks and evaluating possible solutions.

03

Critical Reflection

My confidence improved as I became more comfortable experimenting with technical tasks. I learned that errors and unexpected results are often useful sources of information rather than simply failures.

04

Professional Relevance

Computational thinking provides an important foundation for cybersecurity because effective security analysis depends on structured reasoning and disciplined problem-solving.

05

Next Step

I will continue strengthening my technical confidence through practical cybersecurity exercises, programming and independent experimentation.
